## Differences between the two solutions, and which is right

1. **Placebo.** B is correct. A's placebo is a no-op, and its write-up substitutes numbers that no cell prints. When I rebuilt A's placebo correctly (shifting raw global and country series, rebuilding z-scores and interactions, α=200 as in A), the placebo predictions do differ from real macro (max |Δŷ| 1.0–2.3). The placebo R²_OOS values are −1.30, −2.71, −1.72, −7.00, −2.69, −2.66, −3.45 and −2.46% for s = 36…120. A's real −2.97% sits in the middle of that range. A's *qualitative* Q2 answer happens to be right, but A never produced the evidence for it.
2. **Tuning.** B tunes every penalty in-window with forward folds. A hard-codes its penalties without explanation, and its Q2 magnitude ("catastrophic") depends on that arbitrary choice. B is correct.
3. **Forest vs linear.** B gives both models the same class information. A does not. B's finding (no exploitable nonlinearity, the difference within about 1 SE) is the supported one. A's "linear dominates" is neither fair nor significant.
4. **Q1.** Both solutions' printed numbers show the characteristics add nothing to class means (A: −0.088%, SE 0.070% by my bootstrap; B: −0.045%, SE 0.027%). B says so. A claims "resilient predictability" from one class-level number that is below A's own class-means model.
5. **Schemes and per-class models.** Only B answers "which scheme wins": static is worst (paired t ≈ −1.9 to −2.5), and rolling ≈ expanding. Only B tests per-class against pooled.

## Correct answers for this data

- **Q1: not detectably forecastable.** The pooled R²_OOS of a characteristics model against the pooled trailing mean is about 0.0–0.1% (SE ≈ 0.24%), and it is negative against zero for every specification. On top of class intercepts, the characteristics add −0.05% to −0.09% (SE 0.03–0.07%). The design only has power to detect an R²_OOS of about 0.5%, so the answer is "not detectable", not "zero".
- **Q2: macro adds nothing.** With properly tuned shrinkage the loss is small (−0.12%, SE 0.085%). Lightly shrunk or unpenalised fits lose heavily (OLS −4.7% to −20%; A's α=200, −3.0%). In both B's placebo and my corrected rebuild of A's, real macro falls inside the distribution of placebos shifted 36–120 months.
