## 3. Where the two solutions differ, and which choice is right

- **Placebo.** B is correct: it shifts the raw series before any transform, across all macro. A's placebo is a no-op (A1), so A's Q2 placebo evidence is empty and the reported placebo numbers are not reproducible (A2).
- **Audits.** B's are computations. Several of A's are fixed strings, some wrong (A3), and one key justification comes from a misleading unscaled PCA (A4).
- **x11.** Both drop it after finding the x86 − x12 rebuild. B reports how close the rebuild is per country and how wrong a stale fill would be, which is what the exam asks. A reports one pooled correlation and overclaims exactness.
- **Extended file.** Both use only x86 from it, which is fine. Only B finds the duplicates the exam tells students to find, the hidden global copies, and the frequency classes.
- **Standardisation and mapping.** The two designs are essentially the same and both correct: within-class ranks, differentials against c7 with a trailing z-score and a 2-month lag, no country macro for class A, and x86 in place of x10. B verifies them with tests, and A does not.
- **Know your data.** B covers the full list. A covers roughly a third of it (A6) and quotes unprinted numbers (A7).
- **Leakage.** Neither solution has a fatal leak (no shuffled fold, no pre-split scaler, imputer or PCA in the model, and correct lags). B *demonstrates* causality by truncation. A's compliance on hyper-parameters is unverifiable (A9).
