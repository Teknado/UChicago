# The lecture methods and Problem 3: what was used, what was not, and why

## 1. Purpose and how to read this document

This reference goes through the eight lectures and lists every method, model, test, diagnostic, validation approach and principle they contain, with its status in Problem 3 and the reason, where possible in the form "this approach assumes X, and the data showed Y". It describes the notebook as committed, after the final audit (methodology log §E.7). Every number is printed by the notebook and is cited by table, figure or section (e.g. "Table 3.10", "§3.5"). Where an argument needs a number the notebook does not print, the text says **Evidence gap** and names a proposal (P-A … P-S, listed with costs in §6).

**Scope rule.** Only methods a lecture *teaches* are in scope. Methods a lecture only *names* (in a legend, a list, or printed output) are out of scope, and are marked so.

**Status legend.**
- **USED (primary)**: part of a primary test (Q1–Q3), or a principle or design element the whole study rests on (target, benchmarks, harness, inference, data checks).
- **USED (variant)**: another candidate in the ledger (OLS, lasso, PCR, the forests, per-class ridge), a robustness check, a diagnostic, a pre-listed extra, or a modified form.
- **CONSIDERED & NOT USED**: the notebook, write-up or methodology log gives a reason for leaving it out.
- **NOT USED**: never discussed; the reason given here is ours.
- **NOT APPLICABLE**: ruled out by the problem type or the data.

**"Taught?"** *Taught* = developed on the slides (formula, algorithm, code or worked example); *Principle* = a taught rule or warning; *Example* = a worked illustration; *Named only* = mentioned, not taught (out of scope); *Shown only* = in a figure or printed output without explanation.

**Page numbers** are **PDF pages**, as in the notebook. In Lectures 1, 5 and 8 the printed slide numbers are lower after overlays (L1: footers 1–71 over 82 PDF pages; L5: footer = PDF page − 6 from p.36; L8: footer = PDF page − 4 from p.14, so p.55 is printed "51 / 58").

**Missed opportunity** gives a proposal ID (§6), "Other idea" (listed at the end of §6 with the reason it is low value), or "No". Proposals are not in the notebook: each waits for the student's decision.

## 2. What the data told us, and how that steered the choice of methods

1. **The signal is weak.** The largest $R^2_{OOS}$ of the 38 specifications is 0.21% (SE 0.36%, rf M2 rolling; Table 3.10b). The primary ridge M2 forecast scores 0.042% (SE 0.241%), and no specification beats a zero forecast (Table 3.31). The forecasts have an SD of 0.055 against 1.043 for $y$ and correlate 0.020 with outcomes (§3.5). Ridge chose the no-signal end of its grid in 64% (M2) and 93% (M3) of refits (Table 3.31). *Steer:* shrinkage as the primary method, a grid that reaches the class-means model, decision rules in SE units, a Bonferroni bar of 3.27, a leak alarm at 2%; tuning-heavy learners were unlikely to pay.
2. **No sign of exploitable nonlinearity.** On characteristics alone the forest scores −0.78% / −0.04% / +0.21% against ridge's −0.68% / +0.04% / +0.10% (static / expanding / rolling); with macro, −3.74% / −1.47% / −0.74% against −0.69% / −0.08% / −0.11% (Table 3.10). Deep forests lose to the leaf-200 forest on held-out months by 2.192% (SE 0.358%, M2) and 4.692% (SE 2.409%, M3) (Table 3.21). The hump in the `x3` terciles (0.079 / 0.169 / 0.132, Table 3.6b) never became an out-of-sample gain. *Steer:* one nonlinear check (the forest); boosting, KNN and neural nets had little expected value. Evidence gap: a printed paired forest − ridge difference (P-H).
3. **Assets move together within a month.** Within-class return correlations are 0.21 / 0.55 / 0.73 / 0.58 for A / B / C / D (Table 3.2). A signal common to a whole class (macro) has an effective 8.077 assets, so a true $R^2$ of 0.3% gives t ≈ 1.01, against 3.34 for a within-class ranking signal (§3.0). *Steer:* resample whole months; rank within class so class shocks cancel; no row-wise CV, random splits, or in-sample F-tests and information criteria that treat asset-months as independent; read Q2 from magnitudes and the placebo.
4. **Three of the four classes are tight blocks; class A is not.** B, C and D have within-class correlations of 0.55, 0.73 and 0.58; A's is 0.21, below its 0.24 with class B (Table 3.2; 0.25 against 0.28 in 2003–2010). *Steer:* class intercepts and within-class ranks are well founded for B–D; for A (26 of 50 assets) the class is a loose label, the one thing clustering could check (P-J).
5. **The mean shifted and relations drift.** The mean of $y$ fell from 0.137 (training) to 0.036 (out of sample), so zero beats the pooled trailing mean by 0.32% (Table 3.8). A fit frozen in 2010 does worst: static minus expanding is −0.727% for ridge M2 (t = −1.89) and t = −2.47 for OLS M2 (Table 3.12b). The class-D forecast moves 0.169 → 0.216 → 0.125 (2011, 2015, 2024; Table 3.28b); P1's α is −0.16% (t = −0.37) in 2011–17 and +1.36% (t = 2.47) in 2018–24 (Table 3.27b). *Steer:* expanding windows as primary, rolling as a check, static as a contrast; zero always reported; sub-periods.
6. **Many predictors for few effective observations.** M3 has 42 columns including 3 class intercepts (Table 3.7). Kitchen-sink OLS on M3 fits its window (mean in-sample $R^2$ 7.67%) and scores −20.44% out of sample (static, Table 3.10b); per-class ridge M3 (15 slopes on 672–864 rows per class, §3.5) scores −1.17% against −0.08% pooled (Table 3.17). *Steer:* ridge, lasso and PCR instead of OLS; a pooled model; no country macro for class A.
7. **The data contain look-ahead traps.** `x10` is constant within each year and correlates 0.998 with the same-year mean of `x86` against 0.839 with the prior year's (§3.1, Figure 3.1a). Eight (variable, asset) pairs were back-filled up to 2002-07; `x11` stops early for three countries, where a stale fill would be wrong by up to 10.1 pp; 35 annual and 26 quarterly extended columns have unknown release timing (§3.1). *Steer:* `x86` in place of `x10`; a two-month macro lag; first target month 2003-01; `x11` excluded (gaps, and nearly redundant with `x86 − x12`, rebuild correlation 0.988); the extended file not screened; no outside data; a truncation test (64 columns × 4,800 rows identical, §3.2) and a quarantined leak demonstration (−0.050%, Table 3.22).
8. **Two characteristics are the benchmarks' own inputs.** `x2` is the compounded past 12-month return (99.63% of asset-months) and `x5` the 36-month volatility (99.67%) (§3.1); TSMOM uses sign(R12)/σ̂, RP uses 1/σ̂, and P1 with a constant forecast is exactly RP (§3.3). *Steer:* RP as the natural bar; Q3 needs an α over the three rules; the within-class long–short P3 isolates the cross-sectional bet (gross Sharpe 0.08, net −0.11; Table 3.24). P1's weights correlate 0.935 with RP's (§3.7) and 0.9994 with the class-means portfolio (Table 3.31).
9. **Scales and tails differ enormously.** Monthly volatility runs from 0.82% to 17.40% (§3.1); class A's worst month is −54.7% (Table 3.1a); the 1st percentile of `x3` in class A is −4.13 (Table 3.4). *Steer:* the vol-scaled target, within-class ranks, standardised designs. Evidence gap: sd($y$) by class and by year (P-M).
