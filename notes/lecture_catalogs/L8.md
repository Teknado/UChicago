## Lecture 8 — Trees, Random Forests, and Boosted Trees (BUSN 41210, D. Xiu, 23 Sep 2026; 62 PDF pages, 58 frames)

Lecture 8 has two parts.
- **Part I, one tree (p.3–40).** Tree logic and recursive binary partitions; classification and regression trees (leaf class shares or leaf means); deviance, Gini and sums of squares; greedy recursive CART with stopping rules; cost-complexity pruning $R_\alpha(T)=R(T)+\alpha|T|$ with α chosen out of sample; automatic detection of nonlinearity and interaction; readable versus accurate trees; and "the problem is not bias, it is variance".
- **Part II, many trees (p.41–62).** Bagging, and the random forest's column subsampling; the forest recipe (deep trees, a minimum leaf, B as a budget, "nearly tuning-free"); gradient boosting (shallow trees on residuals, shrinkage ν, rounds B, all tuned on validation data); a horse race on one shared split; "a tuning budget is a leakage risk"; MDI versus held-out permutation importance; partial dependence; a roundup; and a list of named-only learners.
- **Worked examples.** Umbrella toy, a 500-point 2-D toy, NBC pilots, **Social Network Ads (the Problem 1 dataset)**, motorcycle crash data, California housing.

### Citation problems

### Errors

(a) **Errors in what exists.**
1. **The boosting rejection rests on a misapplied reason and is undisclosed (MINOR).**
   - The reason: METHODOLOGY_LOG P3-12 and §F cite p.55's leakage warning. But a GBRT whose B is chosen along `staged_predict` on the same three forward folds carries the same, already-controlled risk as ridge's 36-point α path. The plan designed exactly this (scratchpad `plans/P3_plan.md` l.535). An untuned GBRT has no tuning budget at all.
   - What is sound: runtime, and p.55's "if you have an hour, or a deadline … the forest".
   - The disclosure gap: the exam lists boosted trees in cells 44 and 48, and cell 96's "What we did not try" does not mention them.
   - Fix: add one sentence giving the honest reason, backed by Tables 3.10 and 3.21 (every flexible learner lost).
2. **Cell 96 says characteristic × macro interactions were "not tried" (MINOR).** `rf|M3` and `rf-deep|M3` receive u1..u5 together with un-interacted g_x6..g_x9 and c_* (cell 64 `RF_SETS`). Trees form interactions automatically (p.28; p.37: "recursive splitting *is* interaction"). Those models lost: −1.428% (SE 1.394%) and −3.928% (3.403%) against their M2 versions (Table 3.18). The write-up under-states its own evidence. It should say the forest tried these interactions implicitly and they failed; only the *linear* interaction terms were untried.
3. **Forest in-sample fit read as overfitting evidence (MINOR in P3, NIT in P1).**
   - Cell 96 says the deep forests "fit their training windows with R² of 27% and 58%".
   - P1 cell 17 cites "training-fold accuracy 99.8% for the forest" as the ensembles "chas[ing] noise".
   - A forest's in-sample fit is high by design: the trees are deep and unpruned (p.44), and L8 warns that in-sample forest statistics mislead (p.47, p.56). L8 judges forests only on held-out data (p.45).
   - The valid evidence is already printed: leaf 5 − leaf 200 = −2.192% (SE 0.358%) (Table 3.21), and 88.75% against 90.75% in P1 (Table 1.3). Lead with these; present the in-sample numbers only as context.
4. **The forest's inputs are not in the Methodology (MINOR, reproducibility; exam cell 49 asks that the design be reproducible from the Methodology alone).**
   - The forest does not use the M2/M3 columns of Table 3.7 (8 and 42). It uses `RF_SETS`: 8 and 18 columns, with the class dummies as split variables and macro *not* interacted with class.
   - This appears only in code (cell 64). Cell 70, cell 96's model list and Table 3.7 omit it.
5. **Permutation of one-hot dummies (NIT; my reasoning, not in L8).** Table 3.16 shuffles dB, dC and dD one at a time, which creates impossible rows. Part of their "importance" is extrapolation. Cell 96's sentence "ranks the class dummies and volatility first" needs this caveat, or a grouped permutation of the class label.
6. **"The shallow forest" (NIT; cell 96).** L8 reserves "shallow" for boosting's depth-L learners (p.50–51). The leaf-200 trees have about 10–30 leaves [AUDIT SPOT CHECK from `LOGS['mean leaves per tree']`, which the notebook computes but never displays]. Say "large-leaf forest" instead.
