## Lecture 5 — Regularization for Linear Models: Ridge & Lasso

(Dacheng Xiu, 16 Sep 2026, 60 PDF pages. "L5 p.n" means PDF page n. Slide footers differ after p.28: PDF pp.29–35 are one animated slide, and footer = PDF page − 6 from p.36 on.) The lecture has four parts:
- **Penalised estimation.** It frames estimation as minimising deviance (Gaussian deviance = SSE), explains why OLS becomes unstable as p approaches n (bias–variance), and writes regularisation as penalised MLE, −(2/n) log LHD + λ Σ pen(β_j).
- **Ridge and lasso.** Ridge gets its closed form, the augmented-data view and its coefficient path. Lasso gets its sparsity geometry, soft thresholding, the path algorithm and path plots. The lecture then explains why covariates must be standardised.
- **Choosing λ.** It sets out the parsimony principle and prediction-driven model selection, then how to design an out-of-sample experiment: split size, temporal order, scaling after the split, and keeping the optimum inside the grid. The tools are K-fold CV and the CV-lasso algorithm (demonstrated with LassoCV on three grids and a CV curve), the problems with CV, and AIC/BIC (LassoLarsIC). It also shows that LassoCV leaks through scaling and says to rebuild it on `lasso_path`.
- **Time series and honest reporting.** CV on time series is replaced by expanding and rolling windows, ordered train → validation → test, with code for a 743 × 119 macro panel (180/84/12, step 12) and R²_OOS = 1 − Dev/Dev0 against a trailing mean. The lecture measures what a random fold costs (−0.036 of R²) and lays down the reporting rules: say which benchmark, a negative R² is a result, report the search size, and treat 0.3–0.5% as the sanity range. It closes with the five leakage questions (items 1 and 3 graded fatal), a warning about AI-written code, and when to choose ridge or lasso.

  - The lasso uses sklearn's per-row α, (1/2n)SSE + α‖β‖₁.
  - Ridge is unit-tested against sklearn `Ridge(alpha = a·n)` (cell 72).
- **Standardisation.** A `StandardScaler` is fitted on the fit rows of every fold and every refit, after within-class demeaning (p.23, p.27, p.47, p.59). Scaling by the pooled SD gives each class's interaction slope the same relative shrinkage.
- **Grid.** The grids run from the null model to near-OLS (p.37–39). The lasso grid is descending, so its labels line up with the order `lasso_path` returns (p.21).
- **Validation folds.** There are three annual folds; each fits up to year b−j and validates on year b−j+1. The fold MSEs are averaged (p.35), the order is asserted at every fold (p.49), the test months never enter the tuning loop (p.52), and the model is refit on the whole window (p.36).
- **Schemes.**
  - Static: tuned on 2008–2010 and fit once at 2010-12.
  - Expanding: a fixed start (p.50), refit every 12 months to predict the next 12 (p.51).
  - Rolling: 96 months (p.48).
- **R²_OOS.** It is 1 − SSE/SSE_bench on the pooled 8,400 OOS rows (p.55). The benchmark is a trailing mean through t−1, updated monthly, which is stricter than p.55's single trailing mean. Zero is always reported (p.56–57), and `r2_score` is never used (p.57).
- **Five leakage questions (p.58).** The cell 91 table maps each question in the slide's own wording, and every guard is executable (cells 66, 71, 72, 92).
