## Lecture 1 — Introduction, Data Visualization, and Dimension Reduction

L1 (82 PDF pages; slide footers run 1–71 because of overlays; every citation below and in the notebook uses the **PDF page**) has four parts. (i) Motivation: ML as estimation from data, the three learning paradigms, the AI-investing hype and its failures, and GKX's ML-sorted portfolios (p.5–36). (ii) Why finance is different: low signal-to-noise and market efficiency, short and nonstationary samples, n ≈ p, correlated predictors and unknown nonlinear form, and the "humans were the first machines" list (p.33–35). (iii) Data visualisation: time series, spatial data, the SVD biplot, the scree plot, and covariance/correlation with the `df.corr()` heat map and the "look at the scatter plot" warning (p.37–44). (iv) The one estimator taught in full: **PCA** on the demeaned T × n matrix, with Σ̂ = X′X/T, the eigen-derivation, and Z(k) = XΓk (p.45–74). It ends with named-only examples (t-SNE, text, images; p.75–79) and the course's position on AI tools: framing, validation, judgment, "a number with no benchmark is not a result", and "watch whether the future ends up in the training set" (p.80–82). L1 has no code except `df.corr()`. It has no R²_OOS, no CV and no regression estimator, and gives no rule for choosing k.

### Errors

- **E1 (MINOR, interpretation; P3). PCR on M3 is, in practice, a macro-factor regression, not "dense compression" of characteristics plus macro.**
  - Why: PCA is unsupervised (L1 p.46). It ranks directions by predictor variance (p.63–67), and in M3 the correlated macro × class blocks dominate.
  - Auditor spot check (not printed): in every one of the 14 expanding refits, the retained components (K = 1 twelve times, 2 once, 3 once; cell 77) put at most 0.25% of their squared loadings on u1..u5. In 2003–10, PC1–PC4 load 0.000–0.002 on them.
  - So PCR M3's −0.17% (Table 3.10) and its preference for K = 0 (8 of 14 refits, cell 84) are evidence that *macro factors* add nothing. That supports Q2, not Q1.
  - The write-up lists PCR among the Q1 learners that lose and as "dense compression" (cell 96 §4.1, §5). That is not false, but it misses this. No verdict changes. The code itself is correct: unit-tested against sklearn (cell 72) and fitted inside every fold (cell 71).
- **E2 (MINOR, convention; P3). Correlation-matrix PCA is presented as L1 PCA.**
  - Both the PCR (standardised inputs, cell 71) and Table 3.30 (the `.corr()` matrix, cell 90) use correlation PCA. L1 uses the covariance of centred data with divisor T (p.63).
  - Standardising is the right choice with 20-fold volatility gaps and mixed units. It should be labelled "our choice" in cells 70, 90 and 96, as log P3-19 already does.
  - The divisor (T vs T−1, ddof) changes no eigenvector, so that is not an issue.
- **E3 (NIT; P3). Table 3.30 compares first-eigenvalue shares across windows of different length.**
  - The windows are 96, 108 and 60 months, with n = 50. With T close to n, the top sample eigenvalue is biased upward. L1 p.63 warns only about rank when T < n; the bias itself is outside L1.
  - So 0.29 / 0.26 / 0.30 is not like-for-like. The write-up quotes only the C–D correlation from this table, so no headline is affected.
- **E4 (MINOR, log accuracy).** The scree plot claimed in log P3-19 does not exist (see above).
- **Problems 1 and 2: no conflict with L1.** Cell 24 quotes p.82 correctly. P1 pairs its correlation with a scatter plot (corr 0.155, Figure 1.2b), as p.44 advises. P2 plots the raw return series (Figure 2.4b; p.38).
- **Verified OK (P3).**
  - Demeaning precedes PCA, as L1 requires; the within-class version is stricter.
  - PCA is refit on the fit rows of every validation fold and every window (`n_samples_seen_` is asserted).
