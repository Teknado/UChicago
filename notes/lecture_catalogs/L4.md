## Lecture 4 — Model Evaluation and Selection

Lecture 4 (50 slides, Xiu, 14 Sep 2026) covers evaluation and selection for *linear regression*. Topics: the overall F-test and the partial (nested) F-test, the F = t² equivalence, and how multicollinearity makes the F- and t-tests disagree; why R² always rises and why adjusted R² is "practically useless"; multiple testing, with the false-discovery arithmetic, a live Monte Carlo on 100 pure-noise predictors, and the Bonferroni / max-t critical value (3.65 for 100 tests), leading to "report the size of the search" and "stop asking whether it fits and start asking whether it predicts"; AIC, BIC and BIC model probabilities; subset selection (2^p), correlation screening and forward stepwise selection with AIC, including myopia and instability; the training / validation / test design (validation is a "gray zone"); and in-sample vs out-of-sample R², with the benchmark set to the in-sample Ȳ or to 0 ("it can be negative"). It is illustrated by chronological 80:20 holdouts on the market equity premium (n = 74) and on US IP growth with 119 macro predictors (743 months). The lecture does **not** teach cross-validation, expanding or rolling windows, regularisation, trees, HAC or clustered SEs, or any FDR-control procedure such as Benjamini–Hochberg. It names the false discovery rate only as a concept (p.25).
