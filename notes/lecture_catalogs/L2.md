## Lecture 2 — Linear Regression Models

L2 (106 PDF pages; PDF page = slide number; Dacheng Xiu, 9 Sept 2026) covers simple linear regression (SLR) from start to finish:
- the least-squares line, its closed form (b1 = r·s_y/s_x, b0 = Ȳ − b1X̄) and the residual conditions behind it (p.3–25);
- the ANOVA decomposition and R² (p.26–32);
- the probability model Y = β0 + β1X + ε, ε iid N(0, σ²), and prediction intervals when the parameters are known (p.33–46);
- the single-factor/CAPM application, AAPL on the S&P 500, weekly 2010–11, with α and β as a performance benchmark (p.47–54);
- s² = SSE/(n−p) and degrees of freedom (p.55–58);
- sampling distributions, var(X̄) = σ²/n and the CLT (p.59–77);
- **"What is a standard error, really?"** (p.78–80). The same SE is computed three ways: the formula (0.1116), 10,000 simulated refits (0.1120, with 95.0% CI coverage) and resampling the 104 actual weeks with replacement (0.1190). The conclusion: the textbook SE "assumes errors that are normal, independent, and the same size in every week". "Robust" SEs are only named.
- Student t, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests and p-values, including CAPM tests of α = 0 and of β = 1 by hand (p.81–97);
- prediction intervals with estimation error, and widening away from X̄ (p.98–106).

The house style is `smf.ols`, `ddof=1` and `scipy.stats.t`. There is no multiple-regression inference, no R²_OOS and no CV.
