===== PAGE 24 =====
What is Machine Learning?
Arthur Samuel coined the term Machine Learning in his 1959 paper: “Some studies in
machine learning using the game of checkers.”
Combine expertise from statistics on how to extract information from data with
computational ideas that enable efficient implementation on large data sets.
Machine learning is to big data as human learning is to life experience.
Machine learning mimics human learning.
When a person learns to catch a ball, they do so without first being taught complicated
motion equations. Instead, they practice and learn through repeated trial and error.
16

===== PAGE 25 =====
Machine Learning Paradigms
▶Supervised learning (“predictive analytics”): a class of systems and algorithms that
determine a predictive model using data points with known outcomes. Regression - The model
finds outputs that are real variables; Classification - The model finds classes in which to place its
inputs.
▶Predict Default; Predict Housing Prices; Predict Asset Returns; . . .
▶Unsupervised learning (“exploratory data analysis”): a type of algorithm that learns
patterns from unlabeled data.
▶Recommender system (Amazon - frequently bought together, products you may like,
products related to this item; Netflix - Top Picks for you); Language modeling in NLP;
▶Reinforcement learning: concerned with how intelligent agents make a sequence of decisions
in an uncertain, potentially complex environment. In reinforcement learning, an intelligent agent
faces a game-like situation. The agent employs trial and error to come up with a solution to the
problem. It gets either rewards or penalties for the actions it performs. Its goal is to maximize
the total reward.
▶Alpha Star playing Atari, Starcraft; Alpha Go; Self-Driving Cars; . . .
Watch a free documentary movie of AlphaGo on Youtube to gain some insight about reinforcement learning.

Need for Machines
“Big data” is not just about the size of the data!
▶Historical data in finance (and economics) are typically not large in size, barely
10s of years, 100s of months. Lack of stationarity effectively shrinks the counts
further.
▶Finance does have lots of variables. With n = 100 data points, infeasible to run
linear regressions with p = 100 variables.
▶In cases like this (n ≈p), ML is inevitable.
Why Machine Learning for Finance?
▶Lots of explanatory variables with potentially high correlations
▶Functional form is unknown and likely complex (nonlinear)

What “Correlated ” Means
Two variables are correlated if knowing one tells you something about the
other.
Cov(X, Y ) = E



ρXY = Cov(X, Y )
sd(X) sd(Y ).
When X is above its average, is Y above its average? Multiply the two deviations and average. Its size
depends on the units, so divide by both standard deviations: correlation is covariance in units that
cancel.
▶ρ lies between −1 and +1: +1 a perfect line sloping up, −1 sloping down, 0 no linear relation.
▶A correlation matrix is every pair at once — diagonal 1, symmetric, and a heat map of it is the
fastest way to see structure in a wide dataset. df.corr().
▶ρ = 0 does not mean “unrelated”, only no straight-line relation: a perfect U-shape has ρ ≈0.
Look at the scatter plot as well as the number.
And it is why the next block exists: columns that are highly correlated are not carrying
that many separate pieces of information.

Dimension Reduction
▶X is a dataset with T observations of n variables.
▶Each variable was previously demeaned and write
X = (X 1, . . . , X n),
where X i = (Xi1, . . . , XiT)′, for i = 1, . . . , n.
▶Extract as much information (variance) of X and build another dataset Z with T
observations but only k ≪n variables:
Z = (Z 1, . . . , Z k).

===== PAGE 46 =====
Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
▶PCA is the most common unsupervised learning approach to dimension reduction.
▶PCA is a very versatile tool. It can be applied in many different situation such as:
▶Visualization of large datasets
▶Efficient use of resources ( e.g.: compression)
▶Noise reduction (improving data quality)
▶Pre-treatment of the data for further machine learning algorithms
▶In fact, it can be applied in any situation that requires dimension (number of
features) reduction.
▶It accomplishes such a reduction by “rotating” the dataset in a smart way.

The Math of PCA
▶Let bΣ denote the (n × n) sample covariance matrix of X:
bΣ = 1
T X ′X
Remember: X has been centered.
▶Note that bΣ is symmetric but might rank less than n if, for instance, T < n.
▶The idea is to look for a vector γ ∈Rn such that the sample variance of Xγ is
maximized.

===== PAGE 73 =====
Moving Beyond the First Two Principal Components
▶As you can probably guess by now, we will end up with a solution for the j-th PC
of the form
bΣγj = λjγj,
with γj orthogonal to all γi, i = 1, 2, . . . , j −1.
▶Process repeated until j = n, yielding up to n different eigenvectors γ1, . . . γn of

Z j := Xγj

===== PAGE 74 =====
▶Given the desired number of PCs, say 1 ≤k ≤n, we collect all the vectors
γ1, . . . γk in a (n × k) matrix
Γk := (γ1, . . . γk).
▶The transformed dataset containing only the first k PCs is the (T × k) matrix is
given by
Z (k) := XΓk := (Z 1, . . . , Zk).
▶Also, by construction, the columns of Z (k) (the PCs) are orthogonal random

▶Thus, the sample covariance matrix of Z (k) is given by the (k × k) diagonal matrix
Λk := diag(λ1, . . . , λk).

===== PAGE 81 =====
So What Is Scarce Now?
When the mechanics get close to free, the value moves to the three things that are still
yours:
1. Framing. What is the decision? What would count as an answer? A forecast, a
treatment effect and a trading signal are three different questions about the same data,
and they need three different setups.
2. Validation. How would you know if you were fooling yourself? This is most of what the
next eight lectures are about, and it is the part the machine is worst at, because it
requires knowing where your data came from.
3. Judgment. Is 0.72 good? Compared to what? A tool will tell you it is good. It does not
know your benchmark.
That list is the syllabus. Nine lectures on it — and you should let the machine write
the code the whole way. Lecture 9 closes the loop: the tool itself is assembled from
exactly these parts.

===== PAGE 82 =====
A Live Demonstration — and What to Watch For
We hand one of these tools the macroeconomic panel from this course and ask it to
predict industrial production growth. It will do something impressive. Watch for four
things.
▶Does it ask what the data are? Or does it assume? It cannot see where your file came
from, and it will not usually admit that.
▶How does it split the data? This is a monthly time series. Watch whether the future ends
up in the training set. It usually does.
▶What does it compare the answer to? A number with no benchmark is not a result.
▶What happens when we push back? Tell it the split was wrong and see whether it defends
the answer or folds. Both are informative.
You may use these tools on everything in this course. Lecture 0 and
AI Coding Guide.pdf are about using them well. This slide is about why the rest of
the course still exists.
